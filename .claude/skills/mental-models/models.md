# Mental model library

The lenses the `mental-models` skill chooses from. Each entry gives the question the model
asks and the failure it catches, which is what matters when you apply it. The families follow
the general structure of Shane Parrish's *The Great Mental Models* and Charlie Munger's
"latticework" idea. I added a military planning family from my own experience.

Choose models from at least two families. A model fits only when it changes what I would do.

---

## 1. General thinking

| Model | The question it asks | The failure it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **The map is not the territory** | What does this model, metric or summary leave out of the thing it represents? | Treating a simplification (a yield figure, a spreadsheet, a KPI) as the reality it stands for. |
| **Circle of competence** | Where does the author's knowledge end, and is any part of the argument outside it? | Confident claims in a domain the author or source cannot vouch for. |
| **First principles** | What is actually known to be true here, once inherited assumptions are stripped away? | Reasoning by analogy or convention when the case at hand is different. |
| **Thought experiment** | If one variable changes and everything else stays fixed, what happens? | Untested claims about cause that a simple counterfactual would expose. |
| **Second-order thinking** | And then what? Who responds to the first effect, and how? | Recommendations that work on contact and fail once others adapt. |
| **Probabilistic thinking** | How likely is each outcome, and how confident should we be? | Single-point predictions presented as certainties; ignored tail risk. |
| **Inversion** | What would guarantee failure? What would have to be true for this to be wrong? | Arguments built only forward, never tested against how they could break. |
| **Occam's razor** | Is there a simpler explanation that fits the evidence equally well? | Elaborate mechanisms when a plain one explains the data. |
| **Hanlon's razor** | Is this better explained by error, constraint or incentive than by intent? | Attributing a stakeholder's behavior to bad faith and planning against the wrong threat. |

## 2. Economics and strategy

| Model | The question it asks | The failure it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity cost** | What is given up by choosing this, including the best alternative not taken? | Judging an option in isolation instead of against the next-best use of the same resources. |
| **Marginal thinking** | What does the next unit add or cost, as opposed to the average? | Decisions made on averages when the margin is where the choice is made. |
| **Sunk cost** | Is past spending shaping a decision that should look only forward? | Continuing a course because of what has already been invested. |
| **Incentives** | Who gains and who loses, and how will each of them behave? | Plans that assume people act against their own interests. |
| **Principal–agent** | Does the person acting share the goals of the person they act for? | Delegated decisions that serve the agent rather than the organization. |
| **Externalities** | Who bears a cost or receives a benefit that is not in the price? | Private calculations that omit costs pushed onto others or onto the future. |
| **Tragedy of the commons** | Is a shared, unpriced resource being drawn down because no one owns the limit? | Overuse that is rational for each user and ruinous for all of them. |
| **Comparative advantage** | Who gives up the least to do this, as opposed to who is best at it? | Assigning work to the most capable party instead of the one with the lowest opportunity cost. |
| **Goodhart's law** | Once this measure becomes the target, will it still measure what we care about? | Metrics that get gamed or optimized at the expense of the real goal. |

## 3. Systems

| Model | The question it asks | The failure it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **Feedback loops** | Does the output feed back to amplify or dampen the input? | Linear projections for systems that reinforce or correct themselves. |
| **Bottlenecks** | What single constraint limits the whole system's output? | Improving parts that are not the constraint and seeing no gain. |
| **Margin of safety** | How much can go wrong before the plan fails, and is that buffer large enough? | Plans that work only if every estimate is right. |
| **Scale** | Does what holds at this size still hold larger or smaller? | Extrapolating results across a change in scale that changes the behavior. |
| **Equilibrium** | Where does the system settle once everyone has adjusted? | Treating a temporary state as permanent, or the reverse. |
| **Aggregation** | Does pooling the data hide differences between the parts? | Averages and totals that mask the units actually in trouble. |

## 4. Judgment and bias

| Model | The question it asks | The failure it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation bias** | What evidence would I have looked for if I believed the opposite? | Sources and data chosen because they agree with the conclusion. |
| **Base rates** | How often does this kind of thing usually happen, before the specifics? | Judging a case on its story while ignoring how such cases normally turn out. |
| **Survivorship bias** | What failed and is therefore missing from the evidence? | Lessons drawn only from the cases that lasted long enough to be seen. |
| **Regression to the mean** | Is an extreme result likely to drift back toward normal on its own? | Crediting an intervention for a change that would have happened anyway. |
| **Availability** | Is this judged likely because it is vivid or recent rather than frequent? | Risk estimates distorted by memorable examples. |

## 5. Military planning

| Model | The question it asks | The failure it catches |
|---|---|---|
| **Commander's intent** | If the plan breaks, does everyone still know the purpose and the end state? | Plans so tied to their steps that no one can adapt when conditions change. |
| **Center of gravity** | What is the one source of strength the whole effort depends on, ours or the other side's? | Effort spread across secondary objectives while the decisive point goes unaddressed. |
| **OODA loop** | How fast can we observe, orient, decide and act compared with the environment changing? | Decisions that are correct but arrive after the situation has moved on. |
| **Red team / premortem** | Assume it is a year from now and this failed. What happened? | Optimism that no one with standing has been asked to puncture. |
| **Most dangerous vs. most likely** | What is the most likely course, what is the most dangerous, and is the plan ready for both? | Planning for the expected case only. |
