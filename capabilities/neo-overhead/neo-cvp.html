Executive summary

NeoGenomics cost-volume-profit view, FY2025 · what it means for growth, overhead and the path to GAAP profit

The answer: growth is lifting cash profit fast, but GAAP profit needs ~$958M of sales or ~$43M less fixed cost. Overhead is the cheapest lever.

Decisions needed

  1. Cap cash fixed costs at ~$164M while sales grow, to keep the 4.8× leverage.
  2. Lift the CM ratio through lab supplies and test mix: each +1 pt is ~$8M a year at 2026 sales.
  3. Set a dated path to GAAP profit combining the G&A gap ($28–50M) and stock pay with growth; each $1M of fixed cost cut lowers break-even sales by $3.5M.

CVP briefing

Where NEO sits on the cost-volume-profit chart

FY2025 annual CVP (left) and the quarterly cost data behind the split (right)

Why overhead matters here: with a 28.5% CM ratio, each $1M of fixed cost needs $3.5M of sales to cover it. Cutting the ~$28–50M G&A gap would lower GAAP break-even by ~$100–175M of sales.
Two levers, both needed: grow sales (each +10% lifts adj. EBITDA ~48%) and raise the CM ratio through lab supply and test-mix gains. Sales growth alone needs ~$960M to reach GAAP profit.
