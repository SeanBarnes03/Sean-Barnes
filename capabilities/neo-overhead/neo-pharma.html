Pharma: keep, resize, and set a deadline

Executive summary · NeoGenomics pharma / non-clinical business

Situation

  • Pharma revenue fell from $96M (2023) to $61M over the last 12 months; now 7% of sales, from 16%.
  • The fall is slowing (−27% → -9% year on year) and has held at ~$14.5M a quarter for two quarters. Not yet a bottom.
  • It cost 3.3 pts of 2025 growth and sits beside ~$34M of write-downs and restructuring on non-core assets. Its P&L is not reported.

Why it may still be worth keeping

  • Trial work feeds future clinical tests, NEO’s 15% growth engine.
  • RaDaR needs pharma trials to build evidence for reimbursement.
  • It shares labs NEO pays for anyway: ~$17M a year toward fixed costs at the company margin.
  • Data licensing at near-zero extra cost; no insurer risk; selling now means selling at the bottom.

Pharma briefing

The case for keeping pharma

Six reasons, each with the condition that has to be true

1 · Trial work feeds clinical tests

Tests run in drug trials can become routine clinical tests once the drug is approved. NGS, NEO’s growth engine, grew 22–29%.
Holds if: clinical revenue can be traced to tests that started as trial work.

2 · RaDaR needs pharma

NEO spent $11.3M defending RaDaR in 2025. Sponsor-paid trials build the evidence needed for reimbursement.
Holds if: there is a funded trial pipeline and a reimbursement timeline.

3 · Shares labs NEO pays for anyway

~$58M of revenue at the company’s 28.5% CM covers ~$17M of fixed cost. Exit leaves shared labs, IT and G&A behind.
Holds if: pharma covers the costs that would disappear without it.

4 · Data at near-zero extra cost

Anonymized data from ~1.4M clinical tests a year is licensed to drug companies. It grows without new lab capacity.
Holds if: data revenue grows; it depends on pharma relationships.

5 · Not dependent on insurers

Contract billing with drug companies: no denials or Medicare price cuts, unlike clinical revenue.
Holds if: collections are faster than clinical’s ~80 days (not disclosed).

6 · Don’t sell at the bottom

Trapelo fetched $2.5M after a $15.9M write-down. A sale now could also lock in a goodwill loss.
Holds if: revenue is near the bottom, which the next two quarters will show.
