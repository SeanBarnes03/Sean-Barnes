## 2. Model and figures

**Figure 1 — required.** Reported withdrawals **by aquifer system**, each against its own sustainable-yield line: Kahului, Pā'ia, Makawao and Kama'ole, with the 2023 values and the May 2026 12-MAV distinguished by hatching as reported rather than primary. A sector-level version was drafted and cut: aggregating the four systems puts the sector under its combined 26 Mgal/d yield, which is the opposite of what the paper argues, and the per-system view is the honest one.

*What it has to carry:* that the overdraft is real on every vintage for Kahului, contested for Pā'ia, and small against what this ground once produced.

**Figure 2 — supporting.** Negative-externality diagram: marginal private cost of extraction (pumping cost only) against marginal social cost (pumping cost plus lens depletion and the foregone value to other users), with the unpriced wedge marked at the current withdrawal rate. Built from theory, labeled with the Maui quantities where they anchor it.

**No model workbook.** This engagement produces an argument from published figures, not a computed model. Any arithmetic in the paper — ratios, percentage changes, the 0.1 mgd threshold against Kahului's yield — is shown in the text and checked against the source table.

## 3. Success criteria

A finished paper meets all of these:

1. Documents the pumping-versus-yield position with primary-sourced figures, and states plainly where a figure rests on reporting instead.
2. Reconciles, or explicitly frames as unresolved, the gap between the 2023 withdrawals and the May 2026 moving average.
3. Names and applies all four economic mechanisms — common-pool resource, negative externality, mispricing, and the recharge subsidy — to the Maui facts rather than defining them abstractly.
4. States what the sustainable-yield figures do and do not measure, including CWRM's own return-irrigation caveat and the non-hydrologic boundaries.
5. Evaluates at least three alternatives: designation now, designation at the sector level, and waiting for the USGS study to report.
6. Defends the recommendation against its two strongest objections — the return-flow argument and "why not wait for the study."
7. Recommendation is operational: names who implements it, who is covered, the threshold, the compliance period, and how it is measured and enforced.
8. Includes both figures, each labeled well enough to read away from the text.
9. Fits four pages, double-spaced, 12-point Times New Roman, one-inch margins, excluding title page, figures and bibliography.
10. Carries no repository URL and no identifying information off the title page.
11. Citations and bibliography in Chicago author-date throughout, with legal authorities in their own Bluebook form, which that style permits.
