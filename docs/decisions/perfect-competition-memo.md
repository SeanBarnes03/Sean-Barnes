# Perfect competition — the planting recommendation

I recommend planting 10 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 30 mesclun beds. This mix is projected to generate a season profit of $42,762 because each planted bed adds more revenue than cost, while an eleventh tomato bed would cost more to produce than it would earn.

The key judgment call concerns the carrot and mesclun limits. Both crops remain profitable at their current maximums, meaning the limits—not unfavorable economics—prevent further planting. If either limit can be raised, the carrot limit should be raised first. One additional carrot bed would add approximately $352 in profit, compared with approximately $246 for one additional mesclun bed. Therefore, the business should be willing to pay up to $352 for the right to plant the next carrot bed. That value applies only to the next bed because each additional bed becomes progressively less profitable as its labor requirement rises. Once further carrot beds are no longer profitable, the mesclun limit should be evaluated next.

The farm should not hire another worker or purchase more general farmland under the current plan. It already has four unused beds and approximately 1,203 unused temporary-labor hours, so neither total land nor labor limits production. The binding constraints are the crop-specific limits on carrots and mesclun.

The recommendation is most sensitive to the selling price of tomatoes. Tomato bed 10 has only about a $551 margin between price and marginal cost. A tomato price decline of approximately 7% would make that bed unprofitable and reduce the recommended tomato planting from 10 beds to nine.
